Beyond this module

Adaptive designs, trial sequential meta-analysis and CUSUM charts; software, guidance and further reading.

Adaptive designs

Group sequential designs allow only one kind of change: stopping. Adaptive designs also allow data-driven changes to the sample size, the arms or the population, while still controlling the type I error.

  • Combination tests. The stage-wise p-values are combined with a pre-specified function — Fisher’s product (Bauer and Kohne 1994) or the weighted inverse normal (Lehmacher and Wassmer 1999; Cui et al. 1999). The second stage can then be redesigned freely.
  • Conditional error function. Any modification is allowed if the conditional type I error of the original design is preserved (Proschan and Hunsberger 1995; Müller and Schäfer 2001).
  • Sample-size re-estimation. Unblinded re-estimation, for example the “promising zone” (Mehta and Pocock 2011), and blinded re-estimation or internal pilots (Friede and Kieser 2006). Jennison and Turnbull (2006) show that a well-chosen non-adaptive group sequential design is often as efficient.
  • Multi-arm multi-stage (MAMS), seamless phase II/III and enrichment designs (Magirr et al. 2012).

Reviews and practical guides: Bretz et al. (2009), Pallmann et al. (2018), Burnett et al. (2020) and Wassmer and Brannath (2016). Estimation after adaptive designs is reviewed by Robertson et al. (2023a) and Robertson et al. (2023b).

Trial sequential analysis (sequential meta-analysis)

A cumulative meta-analysis repeats its test whenever a new trial is added, with the same type I error inflation as a monitored trial. Trial sequential analysis applies Lan–DeMets boundaries to the cumulative \(Z\). It uses a required information size adjusted for heterogeneity (Wetterslev et al. 2008, 2017). For statistical methods for updating meta-analyses in living reviews see Simmonds et al. (2017). For an e-value alternative see ALL-IN meta-analysis (Schure and Grünwald 2022).

CUSUM and quality-control charts

The cumulative sum (CUSUM) chart (Page 1954) and its risk-adjusted version for surgical outcomes (Steiner 2000) monitor a process for a change, restarting after each signal. Their error control is expressed as the average run length before a false alarm, not as the type I and II errors of a single test. Reviews: Grigg and Farewell (2004) and Woodall (2006).

Regulatory guidance and reporting

  • ICH E9 requires interim analyses and stopping rules to be pre-specified, with the type I error controlled (International Council for Harmonisation 1998).
  • FDA guidance on adaptive designs treats group sequential designs as a well-understood case (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2019).
  • ICH E20 on adaptive designs was a Step 2 draft in 2025 (International Council for Harmonisation 2025). The EMA reflection paper remains relevant (European Medicines Agency, CHMP 2007).
  • Data monitoring committees: see DeMets and Ellenberg (2016).
  • Reporting: follow the CONSORT extension for adaptive designs (Dimairo et al. 2020).

Further reading

  • Books: Jennison and Turnbull (2000), Whitehead (1997), (2006) and Wassmer and Brannath (2016).
  • Reviews: Todd (2007) (25 years of sequential methodology), Lai (2001), Todd et al. (2001), Whitehead (2004) and Schulz and Grimes (2005).
  • Tutorials for experimental research: Lakens (2014) and Lakens et al. (2021).
